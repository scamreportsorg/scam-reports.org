# The AGPL in plain language

Scam-Reports.org uses `AGPL-3.0-or-later`. This is a practical overview, not legal advice. `LICENSE` controls.

## What it permits

People and organizations may run, study, change, redistribute, and commercially use the software under the license terms.

"Free software" refers to those freedoms. It does not necessarily mean zero price, non-commercial use, free hosting, or charity status.

## When source must be offered

Distributing a covered version carries the AGPL notice and Corresponding Source requirements. Section 13 also applies to modified versions used over a network: users must be offered the Corresponding Source for the version they are using, through the network and at no charge.

Corresponding Source means what is needed to build and modify the covered work, not merely a patch or repository screenshot. Anyone operating a fork should read the full license and get advice for their situation.

## What stays private

The license covers the software. It does not make production databases, private reports, original evidence, account identities, notes, logs, backups, secrets, third-party services, or independent works public. It also grants no rights to the project name or logo beyond a separate license.

Privacy and security duties still apply. Development uses synthetic data, not copied production data.

## Official-site policy

The official service has a separate [free-access policy](FREE_ACCESS.md). The AGPL permits commercial use, so third-party operators are not forced to follow that policy.

## Contributions

Unless a file says otherwise, contributions are `AGPL-3.0-or-later` and need a DCO sign-off. Contributors keep their copyright while licensing the work to the project. Dependencies and other third-party material keep their licenses. Required notices are in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
